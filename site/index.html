How a trail works

Short enough to finish, spaced enough to stick.

The whole design assumes a child who has ten minutes before dinner and no patience for a loading screen.

Pick a trail

Six tiers of sixty-four words, or this week's list from school. Each trail draws from the pool, so replaying a tier gives a mostly different set.

Walk it

Eight or so questions, each in the mode that suits the word. Recall checkpoints — where the whole word has to be produced from scratch — are spaced evenly rather than saved for the end.

Miss one? It comes back.

Once before the trail ends, one step easier. Then it waits at review camp until it's been answered cleanly twice.

Five ways to ask

The same word, asked five different ways.

Recognising a word and producing it are different skills. A trail exercises both, and steps down to an easier mode rather than showing a question that can't be answered.

🔊

Listen & spot

RECOGNITION

Hear the word, then pick it from three spellings — two of which are plausible mistakes a child would actually make, not obvious non-words.

🧩

Fill the gap

STRUCTURE

STRUCTURE

Tap every chunk in order. A long word stops being one intimidating shape and becomes three small ones.

🧠

Memory trail

RECALL CHECKPOINT

The word is shown for a few seconds — longer words get longer, up to five — then hidden, and asked for from memory.

⌨️

Typing checkpoint

RECALL CHECKPOINT

No options, no letters on screen. Just the word spoken aloud and an empty box.

Review camp

A word can be right ten times and still have been missed this morning.

That's why review camp doesn't track a total score. A word joins the moment it's missed and only leaves after two clean answers since the mistake — so getting it right in the same trail counts towards getting out, which is the whole point.

The queue is ordered neediest-first: fewest clean answers since the miss, then the freshest mistake. A review trail takes from the front of that list rather than sampling it.

🔁 Missed → back in the same trail One step easier than the mode that caught it.
⛺ Then it waits at camp Shown on the trail map only when there's something in it.
✅ Two clean answers to leave Second looks count for review, but never for the streak or accuracy.
🤷 "I don't know" on every question An honest out beats a wild guess, and it's scored as a miss either way.
Privacy

A children's app that collects nothing.

Not "collects nothing important" — there is no account to collect it against. Progress lives in the browser on the device, and that's the whole system.

🚫 No accounts, no sign-upNo email, no password, no profile on a server. Nothing to delete because nothing was made.
📵 No ads, no trackersNo third-party scripts. Even the fonts are self-hosted so they can't phone home.
💾 Backup is opt-inA grown-up asks for it and gets a code like otter-sequoia-thicket-3341. That code is the only key — and the app says so plainly.
🗑️ Deletable, and it expiresBackups can be removed from the same panel, and untouched ones expire after two years.
For grown-ups

Set it up once, then stay out of the way.

This week's spelling list

Paste it in, one per line or comma-separated. Use word: sentence to supply your own sentence. The app syllabifies each word and generates its decoys, so a bare list still plays every mode.

Up to six players

One device, six children, separate progress, badges, word lists, and companions. Each has their own trail length — 3, 5, 8, 12, or 16 words.

Pick a better voice

Browsers default to the oldest, most robotic voice installed. Spell Trail scores the available ones and picks the best — and you can override it.

Nothing is locked

Fireflies buy cosmetic trail companions and nothing else. No word, tier, or feature is ever gated behind a currency or a purchase.

Open source, on purpose

The code is on GitHub. All of it.

Anyone can point an AI at a product and say "make me one of these," so pretending the code is a secret seems like the wrong bet. We'd rather build in the open, be checkable about the privacy claims on this page, and compete on the experience.
